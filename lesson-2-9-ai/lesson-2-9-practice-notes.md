# Which AI tools you used and how the choice depended on the task

I chose Copilot when I was working directly in Excel because it could generate formulas and help me work within the workbook. For verification, I used Claude because it helped me explain the results in more detail and provided additional perspectives that Copilot did not always provide.

# The interaction where AI was most useful and why

The most useful interaction for me was explaining complex formulas. AI helped me understand how the formula worked step by step and showed me alternative formulas that could accomplish the same task in a simpler way. Understanding the formula is important because if another team member asks me about it, I can explain how it works even if I did not originally create the formula myself.

# The interaction where AI was least useful or required the most verification

I don't think AI was least useful for any particular task, but formula generation required the most verification. As an analyst, I think it is important to understand how to create formulas yourself first. AI can then be used to verify or improve your work. If you rely on AI to generate the formula before understanding the problem, it may be difficult to know whether the formula actually matches what you are trying to accomplish.

# One pattern you noticed across the five interactions (e.g., "AI is consistently strong at formula generation, weaker at pivot summarization")

I noticed that AI was generally strong at generating formulas, but it was less consistent when summarizing information and selecting the most appropriate chart. Different AI tools may recommend different chart types for the same data, so the analyst still needs to understand the data and decide which visualization communicates the information best.

# One sentence on how you'd refine your AI use if you redid the engagement

If I did this engagement again, I would focus on understanding which AI tool to use and when to use it, while always verifying the results before reporting them.
